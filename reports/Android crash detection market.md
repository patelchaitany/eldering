# Non-Pixel Android leaves crash detection wide open

Smartphone car crash detection is now a free baseline on iPhone (on by default since the iPhone 14 in 2022) and on Google Pixel (opt-in, 21 countries). It is a freemium funnel at Life360 and a B2B insurance product run largely by Cambridge Mobile Telematics (CMT) and Arity. Most Android phones outside China, including Samsung, Motorola, OnePlus, Vivo and Oppo, still have no OS-level equivalent, and that is the clearest opening for a new app. "Smartphone crash detection" has no clean market size of its own. It sits inside adjacent markets: automatic crash notification (about USD 3-6B), personal safety apps (about USD 1-2B) and usage-based insurance (USD 30-75B). The money that has actually changed hands flows through insurers, not app-store subscriptions. Technically, an accelerometer threshold alone produces about 10 false alarms per driving hour. Gating on vehicle context and fusing several sensors with a small learned model cuts that to about 0.5 per hour, which is still too many for unconfirmed emergency calls, so a user countdown is mandatory. Android also sets hard limits: a third-party app **cannot auto-dial 911/112**. It can auto-text personal contacts under a narrow Play Store exception, it must run as a declared foreground service, and it must survive aggressive OEM battery killers. The recommended MVP therefore has these parts:

- it works on any Android phone;
- it alerts contacts first, sending a free automatic SMS plus a one-tap emergency dial;
- it uses drive-gated sensor fusion that accounts for accelerometer clipping;
- it has a multilingual 30-60 second countdown;
- it walks users through each OEM's battery settings;
- it processes sensor data on the device.

The strongest ways to stand out are two-wheeler detection for India and Southeast Asia, regional languages, SMS fallback when there is no data, and never charging for emergency escalation. Revenue would come from insurers, fleets and gig platforms rather than consumers.

## Apple and Pixel made crash detection free, but only on their own hardware

Apple's Crash Detection is the benchmark. It shipped in September 2022 on iPhone 14 and Apple Watch Series 8, SE 2 and Ultra, and it is **on by default** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104959)). It detects severe front, side, rear-end and rollover crashes in passenger cars. When it fires, the device alarms and shows an alert, and the iPhone places an emergency call after about 20 seconds unless the user cancels ([Apple iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/manage-crash-detection-iph948a628e9/ios)). On the Watch, a 30-second countdown follows the 10-second alert ([Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/manage-crash-detection-apd3a60b5576/watchos)). The call plays a recorded message with latitude, longitude and a search radius, then texts emergency contacts. If there is no cellular or Wi-Fi coverage, it falls back to satellite. It deliberately **defers to a vehicle's own eCall or OnStar system** rather than placing a duplicate call ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/104959)).

Google's Car Crash Detection, part of the free Personal Safety app, is Android's reference implementation, but it is narrow. It runs on Pixel 4a and later (the help page is inconsistent and elsewhere says Pixel 3), plus Pixel Watch paired with a Pixel phone. It is opt-in, needs location, physical-activity and microphone permissions, and works only with a SIM from the country the phone is in: **not while roaming, not in Airplane mode and not with Battery Saver on**. It is available in 21 countries and 12 languages, with no Hindi, Indonesian, Thai, Vietnamese, Brazilian Portuguese or Arabic ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)). Its flow is deliberately slow. The phone vibrates, sounds an alarm and asks aloud whether the user needs help, and the user has **60 seconds** to say "Cancel" or tap "I'm OK". With no response, it calls emergency services on speakerphone, plays a crash message with approximate location through Android Emergency Location Service, and can start Emergency Sharing, which sends contacts a live location for 24 hours ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)). Users can also mark a false trigger as "minor crash" or "no crash", which gives Google labeled training data at almost no cost ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2022/09/07/google-pixel-car-crash-detection/)).

Other Android makers have mostly not followed. Samsung's Galaxy S24 and S25 firmware contains a dormant "car crash detection" virtual sensor that is not enabled anywhere ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-galaxy-s25-car-crash-detection-sensor-3511840/)). A February 2026 blog claimed the Galaxy S26 ships with the feature ([UBOS](https://ubos.tech/news/samsung-galaxy-s26-adopts-pixels-advanced-safety-features/)), but April 2026 user threads say it does not ([Samsung Community](https://us.community.samsung.com/t5/Galaxy-S26/Crash-Detection-Help/td-p/3534206)), and there is no official Samsung announcement. Samsung's watches offer only fall detection, which has a 60-second window ([Samsung UK](https://www.samsung.com/uk/support/mobile-devices/how-to-set-up-fall-detection-on-the-galaxy-watch/)). Xiaomi added crash detection that calls China's 120 emergency number in mid-2025, in China only ([Gizchina](https://www.gizchina.com/2025/07/16/xiaomi-just-taught-your-phone-to-call-for-help-after-a-crash/)). The notes found no citable crash feature from OnePlus, Motorola, Vivo, Oppo or Honor.

Third-party products fill the gap in three ways. **Life360** is the main cross-platform consumer competitor. It detects crashes above **25 mph** and alerts the user's Circle for free, but live-agent emergency dispatch requires a paid tier ([Life360 blog](https://www.life360.com/blog/crash-detection)). Its US tiers are Silver at $9.99, Gold at $16.99 and Platinum at $24.99 a month ([Life360 Plans & Pricing](https://www.life360.com/plans-pricing)). Sources conflict on whether Silver includes dispatch ([Life360 Help](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053410919063-Life360-Free-Crash-Detection)). **OnStar Guardian** brings GM's advisor model to any car for about $15/month, covering up to seven family members ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1480411487)). **Insurer apps** give crash detection free to policyholders. Progressive's Accident Response launched in November 2024 and had more than 1.5M users when it moved to CMT's DriveWell Fusion in November 2025 ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/11/21/cambridge-mobile-telematics-ai-platform-now-paired-with-progressives-accident-response/)). State Farm piloted collision detection in Illinois and Florida in October 2025 ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/10/06/state-farm-introduces-collision-detection-in-its-mobile-app/)). Behind these sits a small group of vendors: CMT served 21 of the 25 largest US auto insurers after acquiring TrueMotion ([Aftermarket News](https://www.aftermarketnews.com/cambridge-mobile-telematics-acquires-truemotion/)), Arity licenses Allstate's technology, and Zendrive went to Intuit in 2024 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/zendrive/financials)). Niche apps cover riders. Liberty Rider bundles automatic accident detection into a €3.99/month Premium tier that partner insurers such as AXA and MACIF give away ([Liberty Rider](https://liberty-rider.com/)). REALRIDER charges about £3.99/month and connects to UK 999 services ([BAPCO](https://www.bapco.org.uk/what_we_do/999apps/realrider.aspx)). Bosch Help Connect routes phone- or bike-detected crashes through a Bosch service center ([Motorcycle News](https://motorcyclenews.com/news/new-tech/bosch-accident-detection-system)).

## Insurers, not consumers, pay for crash detection

No research firm sizes "smartphone crash detection" as its own category, so every figure is a proxy, and the proxies disagree. Automatic crash notification is estimated at USD 6.1B in 2024, rising to USD 14.6B by 2033 ([Growth Market Reports](https://growthmarketreports.com/report/automatic-crash-notification-system-market)). A narrower "emergency notification in automobiles" estimate is USD 2.9B in 2025 ([Research Nester](https://www.researchnester.com/reports/emergency-notification-system-in-automobile-market/3220)). Personal safety apps are estimated at USD 1.76B in 2025, growing 15.4% a year ([Coherent via GII](https://www.gii.tw/report/coh1863993-personal-safety-app-market-by-application-by.html)), or as low as USD 0.98B ([Global Growth Insights](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/personal-safety-app-market-102259)). The large pools are usage-based insurance, at USD 30.6B in 2023 by one estimate ([MarketsandMarkets](https://marketsandmarkets.com/ResearchInsight/size-and-share-of-usage-based-insurance-market.asp)) and USD 75B in 2025 by another ([IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/usage-based-insurance-market)), and fleet telematics at about USD 10.4B in 2025 ([MarketsandMarkets via press release](https://smb.orangeleader.com/article/Fleet-Telematics-Market-to-Grow-from-USD-1042-billion-in-2025-and-to-reach-USD-2195-billion-by-2032-at-a-CAGR-of-112percent-MarketsandMarketstm/6a88502de2557832e11105d1)). The clearest valuation signal is CMT itself. It reportedly raised **USD 350M from TPG Rise and Allianz X** in 2026 and reaches more than 55M drivers in 25 countries ([EBSCO summary of Fortune](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/engineering/391dc52a-e6c7-52db-81e5-263a53846b85/exclusive-cambridge-mobile-telematics-secures-350-million-from-tpg-allianz-to-make-driving-safer)). It also says its AI total-loss product saves insurers about USD 1,500 per claim ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220906005301/en/Cambridge-Mobile-Telematics-Launches-Next-Generation-Platform-for-Proactive-Claims)). Investors value phone crash data mainly as a claims and underwriting asset.

The human need is larger than any of these figures. Roads kill **1.19M people a year**, and more than half of them are pedestrians, cyclists and motorcyclists ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-12-2023-despite-notable-progress-road-safety-remains-urgent-global-issue)). India recorded **172,890 deaths in 2023, 44.8% of them two-wheeler riders** ([Siasat/PTI](https://www.siasat.com/1-72-lakh-people-died-in-road-accidents-in-2023-road-ministry-report-3264450/)), and about 68.5% of them in rural areas ([Moneylife/IndiaSpend](https://www.moneylife.in/article/why-9-bike-riders-die-every-hour/78795.html)). The US recorded 39,345 deaths in 2024 ([NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/nhtsa-estimates-39345-traffic-fatalities-2024)) and the EU 19,940 ([European Commission](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/eu-road-fatalities-drop-3-2024-progress-remains-slow-2025-03-18_en)). Automatic crash notification can cut crash-to-EMS notification time to about one minute ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3256762/)). In India, a SaveLIFE Foundation survey found 74% of bystanders unlikely to help a badly injured victim, mostly for fear of police and court hassle ([SaveLIFE Foundation](https://savelifefoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/NATIONAL-STUDY-ON-IMPACT-OF-GOOD-SAMARITAN-LAW.pdf)). Where bystanders won't call, an automatic alert stands in for them.

The industry has settled on four business models. OEMs (Apple, Google, Xiaomi) give crash detection away to differentiate their platforms. Consumer apps charge about $4-25 a month. B2B vendors (CMT, Arity, DriveQuant, Sentiance) license SDKs and earn from insurers' claims savings and usage-based pricing. In the EU, regulation simply mandates eCall. Consumers resent paying for emergency features. A widely shared post accused Life360 of "putting a paywall in front of emergency services" ([r/assholedesign](https://www.reddit.com/r/assholedesign/comments/q8i848/life360_putting_a_paywall_in_front_of_emergency/)), and Life360 now advertises basic crash alerts with "no subscriptions, no fees" while still charging for dispatch ([Life360 Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/Life360/videos/crash-alerts-on-us/1891260371520758/)). For a new Android entrant, this means emergency escalation should be free. A US price of $17-25 a month will not work in India or Southeast Asia. The realistic revenue routes are insurers, which can use crash detection to start a claim automatically (first notice of loss), two-wheeler and EV makers' apps, gig and delivery platforms with duty-of-care obligations, and fleets. Liberty Rider's insurer-subsidized distribution in Europe is a working template ([Banque Populaire](https://www.banquepopulaire.fr/assurer/rouler-deux-roues-serenite-liberty-rider/)).

## Accelerometer thresholds alone fail; gated multi-sensor fusion works

Every credible system, academic or commercial, first confirms the phone is in a moving vehicle and only then looks for an impact, and it requires more than one sensor to agree. The accelerometer is necessary but unreliable on its own because phone sensors saturate. Crash tests by AGH University with the insurer PZU logged 20 Android models whose per-axis limits were **16g, 4g or 2g**, and found that lower-range phones "clip" during impacts ([Paciorek et al., ICCS 2021](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2021/papers/127470294.pdf)). By contrast, Apple put a **256g** accelerometer in the iPhone 14 because forces at the phone can exceed 100g ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/10/apple-offers-a-deeper-dive-into-crash-detection/)). Worse, ordinary driving in the AGH data produced higher accelerometer readings than a 20 km/h crash, from phone handling, floor drops and door-pocket slams. The useful crash signal spanned 1-3 seconds, of which **200-400 ms was actual contact** ([Paciorek et al.](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2021/papers/127470294.pdf)). The other sensors each add something but none is decisive alone. The gyroscope captures rollovers, but a loose phone spins more than the car. GPS speed sets the speed gate and confirms the sudden stop. In Google's hard-braking research, a GPS heuristic beat an accelerometer heuristic by a wide margin ([Liu et al., Google Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01934)). The microphone hears airbags, which peak at 160-180 dB, but phone microphones clip around 145 dB, the same level reached by shouting or a loud radio with the windows open ([WreckWatch](https://www.dre.vanderbilt.edu/~schmidt/PDF/wreckwatchj.pdf)). Apple uses the barometer to catch the cabin pressure spike when airbags deploy, though open windows can mask it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/10/apple-offers-a-deeper-dive-into-crash-detection/)). Many budget Android phones have no barometer, so it can only ever be an optional confirming signal.

The academic record measures how much fusion helps. WreckWatch (Vanderbilt, 2011) used a 15 mph speed gate, about a 4g impact threshold, a 140 dB sound check and a 10-second cancel window. It published no accuracy numbers, and its 4g threshold dates from phones that saturated near 2g ([WreckWatch](https://www.dre.vanderbilt.edu/~schmidt/PDF/wreckwatchj.pdf)). AGH's measurements show the real cost of simple thresholds. At 80% sensitivity, a threshold detector produced about **10 false positives per driving hour**. A depth-3 decision tree over eight windowed accelerometer features cut that to about 0.6 per hour, and adding the gyroscope cut it to about 0.5. The authors concluded that a credible detector cannot rely on accelerometer thresholds alone, and that the system must check with the driver before raising an alarm ([Paciorek et al.](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2021/papers/127470294.pdf)). The largest public benchmark, Verizon Connect's 2026 **VZCrash**, has 31,090 verified crashes from 100 Hz dashcam IMUs. A learning-free peak-acceleration baseline scored 89.6% average precision (AP), and 1-2M-parameter CNN-RNN and CNN-Transformer models scored about 97.5%. On real fleet data, where crashes were under 0.02% of harsh-driving events, the best model's AP fell to about 86%. At 75% recall, the deep model raised 10 false positives where the baseline raised 780. Trained on only 1-5% of the data, however, the deep models did *worse* than the baseline ([VZCrash, arXiv 2606.06074](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06074v1)). The lesson for a startup is that the amount of labeled data matters more than model architecture, so the app should start with rules plus a small tree model and move to deep models as labeled data accumulates. Vendor claims are thin: The Floow reports more than 80% precision and recall ([The Floow](https://www.thefloow.com/latest/the-floow-crash-detection-service-exceeds-80-in-precision-and-recall-and-is-foundational-to-the-floowclaims-product-suite/)), and CMT publishes no figures.

The main false-alarm sources are well catalogued: phone drops and tosses, door slams, potholes and speed bumps, railway crossings, hard braking, and non-driving activities with high g-forces such as roller coasters and skiing ([Damoov](https://dev.to/damoov/crash-detection-algorithms-on-smartphones-how-to-minimize-false-positives-in-telematics-apps-4ilh); [Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/roller-coasters-basic-clumsiness-are-triggering-the-ip-1849637474)). The working defense is a stack of checks that an event must pass in turn:

1. **Speed before impact.** The vehicle was moving above about 15 km/h.
2. **Duration.** Drops last milliseconds, while collisions last hundreds of milliseconds.
3. **Multiple axes.** Force shows up on two or more axes.
4. **Behavior after the event.** If the vehicle carries on at highway speed after a spike, reject it.
5. **Rotation.** The phone's rotation is consistent with an impact ([Damoov](https://dev.to/damoov/crash-detection-algorithms-on-smartphones-how-to-minimize-false-positives-in-telematics-apps-4ilh)).

Apple adds "no single silver bullet" logic: the signals it requires depend on how fast the car was going earlier ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/22/10/10/apple-says-theres-no-single-silver-bullet-behind-crash-detection)). Models are small enough to run on the phone. VZCrash's CNN-Transformer has 1.3M parameters and ran in 7.2 ms ([VZCrash](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06074v1)), and comparable INT8 TFLite motion models are about 113 KB ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/raj5517/imu-activity-classifier/commit/c80ca4e7ecede2ee4a87ecbe70e5a686e480f628)).

Data is the binding constraint. **No public dataset pairs real-crash smartphone sensor data with negatives such as phone drops.** VZCrash uses rigidly mounted dashcam IMUs, not loose phones, and its license is unconfirmed. The Kaggle smartphone dataset uses *simulated* crashes ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/drabdulbari/smartphone-imu-road-accident-detection-dataset)). IEEE DataPort and phone fall-detection datasets supply only negatives ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/smartphone-imu-and-gps-dataset); [figshare](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Dataset_for_Mobile_Phone_Sensing_Based_Fall_Detection/1444405)). The incumbents built their positive examples expensively. AGH staged crash tests with an insurer. Apple used crash labs with thousands of devices. CMT crashes cars and compares its readings against Bosch's crash data retrieval (CDR) tool ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/10/apple-offers-a-deeper-dive-into-crash-detection/); [CMT](https://www.cmtelematics.com/blog/video-crashing-cars-for-accurate-connected-claims-data/)). A new entrant can get started three ways: replay VZCrash traces with synthetic clipping at 2, 4, 8 and 16g, partner with an insurer for crash tests, and collect its own drop, door-slam and pothole negatives at scale.

## False alarms and paywalls drive the loudest complaints

The feature does save lives. In 2025, an iPhone called 911 for an unconscious teenager trapped after she fell asleep at the wheel ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/iphone-crash-detection-saves-teen-asleep-driving/amp/)), and a Pixel dialed 911 for the unconscious driver of a loader that rolled into a ravine ([Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/news/google-pixel-hidden-feature-saved-a-mans-life-heres-how-to-activate-it)). The backlash has been equally concrete. One weekend, Summit County, Colorado, received **71 automated crash calls from skiers, none of them real** ([Appleosophy](https://appleosophy.com/2023/01/14/crash-detection-also-causes-false-911-calls-from-skiers/)). Cook County, Minnesota, logged 155 false calls triggered by snow activities ([Cook County](https://www.facebook.com/CookCounty/posts/snow-accidents-trigger-fake-911-calls-through-apple-crash-detectionapple-iphones/577547644413785/)), and small resort towns sometimes have a single dispatcher sorting them ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/apple-crash-detection-software-triggers-fake-911-calls)). Apple shipped two rounds of "optimizations" in iOS 16.1.2 and 16.3.1 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/02/13/ios-16-3-1-iphone-14-crash-detection/)). Even so, responders in Pitt County, North Carolina, said in October 2025 that phone crash alerts had brought more false alarms than real emergencies that year ([WITN](https://www.witn.com/2025/10/07/pitt-county-first-responders-see-surge-false-alarms-phone-crash-alerts/)). Any app that robocalls emergency services takes on that hostility.

Consumer complaints fall into five groups:

1. **False alerts.** Arlo Safe users reported repeated crash alerts while sitting at home ([Arlo community](https://community.arlo.com/t5/Arlo-Safe/REPEATED-FASLSE-CRASH-DETECTION/m-p/1889744)).
2. **Missed crashes.** One example is a "Crash detection does not work" thread on r/Life360 ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Life360/comments/1kikllb/crash_detection_does_not_work/)). Life360 also says it can miss fender-benders and needs Power Saving off ([Life360 Help](https://support.life360.com/hc/en-us/articles/23053410919063-Life360-Free-Crash-Detection)).
3. **Paywalled dispatch.** Users object to paying for the part of the feature that summons help.
4. **Battery drain.** Greenlight cites about 2% per hour of driving ([Greenlight](https://help.greenlight.com/hc/en-us/articles/9323051701147-What-is-the-impact-of-crash-detection-on-my-phone-s-battery)).
5. **Platform and country lock-in.** Pixel's feature fails when roaming or on a foreign SIM, and India was added in late 2023 without any Indian language ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/10/31/google-pixel-car-crash-detection-new-countries/); [Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)).

An academic review names false positives and battery use as the two central engineering challenges for crash-alert apps ([MATEC](https://www.matec-conferences.org/10.1051/matecconf/202439201080)).

The most underserved users are two-wheeler riders in India and Southeast Asia. Car-focused OEM features and Life360's 25 mph floor are not designed for them, and they account for nearly half of India's road deaths. In 50 large Indian cities, two-wheeler riders, cyclists and pedestrians were 74% of road deaths in 2023 ([Moneylife/IndiaSpend](https://www.moneylife.in/article/why-9-bike-riders-die-every-hour/78795.html)). Sentiance extended its crash detection to motorcycles in 2024 ([Mobility Outlook](https://mobilityoutlook.com/features/sentiance-unveils-motorcycle-crash-detection-system-to-slash-road-fatalities)), but the notes found no evidence it is deployed in India, and no Indian two-wheeler maker offering crash SOS. Gig and delivery riders, older drivers whose families want alerts, and drivers in rural areas with weak coverage are further gaps. The research could not size them.

## Android lets a third-party app detect a crash but not dial 911

The most important platform fact is that `ACTION_CALL` "cannot be used to call emergency numbers". A third-party app can only use `ACTION_DIAL`, which opens the dialer with the number filled in and leaves the user to press Call ([Android Intent reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/content/Intent#ACTION_CALL)). Pixel can auto-call only because it is a privileged system feature. An app *can* automatically text personal emergency contacts. To do so it must file Google Play's Permissions Declaration under the exception "Physical safety/emergency alerts to send SMS", which grants `SEND_SMS` only and only where "there's currently no alternative method." "Family or device locator" is explicitly not a valid reason ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en)). Sending SMS from a server avoids that permission but needs a data connection and carrier registration: US 10DLC registration, without which "texts will be blocked" ([Twilio](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/13718729624091-Required-information-for-United-States-A2P-10DLC-registration)), and India's TRAI DLT template registration ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/registrations-sms-senderid-india-register.html)). US law also favors keeping a human between the app and the emergency call center. The TCPA bars autodialed or prerecorded calls to 911 lines except for "emergency purposes" ([47 USC 227](https://m.openjurist.org/47/usc/227)). The FCC's PSAP Do-Not-Call registry imposes fines of **$10,000-100,000 per call** for autodialing registered PSAP numbers ([FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-adopts-rules-protect-emergency-service-providers-robocalls)). NENA, the US 911 professionals' association, asks developers to say clearly whether their app dials 911 directly ([NENA](https://www.nena.org/page/smartphoneapps)). Fully automatic emergency contact therefore needs a live-agent monitoring partner, as OnStar does in the US and as third-party-service (TPS) eCall providers do in the EU ([CEPT ECC](https://cept.org/files/2099/Draft%20revision%20of%20ECC%20Recommendation%20(17)04.docx)).

Always-on sensing is possible but must be engineered carefully. On Android 9 and later, background apps receive no continuous accelerometer or gyroscope events, so detection must run in a foreground service. Apps targeting Android 12 or later are capped at **200 Hz**, and if the user turns off the microphone privacy toggle, motion sensors are rate-limited regardless of permissions ([Android Sensors overview](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_overview)). Android 14 requires each foreground service to declare a type, and the Play Console requires a feature description and a demo video for each type ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821?hl=en)). The `location` type fits driving best. The `health` type is documented for fitness use, so a car-safety app risks pushback in review. `specialUse` depends entirely on the reviewer ([FGS types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)). An Activity Recognition `IN_VEHICLE` transition is an explicit exemption that lets an app start a foreground service from the background ([FGS background-start restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)), so automatic drive detection works. However, a `location` service started this way only gets location if the app holds `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`. Play allows background location for "physical safety", but requires a prominent disclosure before the permission prompt and a video of 30 seconds or less ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150?hl=en)). A `microphone` foreground service cannot start at boot and generally cannot start from the background, so Pixel-style audio confirmation is realistic only for drives the user starts manually. Since January 2025, full-screen alerts such as a crash countdown need a special permission the user grants by hand ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821?hl=en)). Google's own Doze guidance lists "Safety app" as an acceptable reason to request a battery-optimization exemption ([Doze and App Standby](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)). That exemption matters because Samsung, Xiaomi, Huawei and OnePlus get the worst score (5/5) for killing background apps ([dontkillmyapp.com](https://dontkillmyapp.com/)), and those are the phones the app targets.

Battery cost is manageable if sensing is gated to drives. In one study, the accelerometer roughly tripled idle battery drain while GPS raised it 9-13x ([arXiv 1908.10725](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.10725)). Using the accelerometer to decide when to sample GPS cut GPS use by 78% while keeping 91.4% accuracy ([KFUPM](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/energy-efficient-adaptive-gps-sampling-using-accelerometer-data/)).

Regulation shapes the market in three ways:

- **EU eCall reduces the need in newer cars.** Every new EU car type since March 2018 carries an automatic 112 crash call (eCall) ([Regulation (EU) 2015/758](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2015/758/introduction/data.html)), and all emergency call centers must support the 4G/5G version by 1 January 2026 ([EENA](https://eena.org/our-work/eena-special-focus/ecall/)). The EU opportunity is therefore older cars, motorcycles and cyclists.
- **Privacy rules restrict how location data can be used.** The EDPB's connected-vehicle guidelines single out geolocation and call for data minimisation and early deletion ([EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu:443/sites/default/files/consultation/edpb_guidelines_202001_connectedvehicles.pdf)). India's DPDP Act obligations take effect around May 2027, with penalties up to INR 250 crore ([Uniqus](https://uniqus.com/digital-personal-data-protection-act-timelines/)). Texas's 2025 lawsuit against Allstate and Arity over covert driving-data collection shows the litigation risk of reusing telemetry for other purposes ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/10/06/state-farm-introduces-collision-detection-in-its-mobile-app/)).
- **Medical-device rules are probably not triggered.** An app that only alerts contacts is unlikely to count as a medical device. This is an unverified inference, and adding injury triage could change it.

## A contacts-first, any-Android MVP has room to win

The evidence points to an MVP that turns Android's main restriction, that apps cannot auto-dial emergency numbers, into a design that is easier on dispatchers. It alerts trusted contacts first and automatically, makes calling 112 or 911 a single tap, and adds a live-agent relay to emergency services later through a partner. The table below lists the features and the evidence behind each.

| MVP feature | What it does | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| Drive gating | Activity Recognition `IN_VEHICLE` enter/exit starts and stops a `location` foreground service; optional car Bluetooth tag; manual "Start drive" | Exempt background start; saves battery; context gating rejects roller-coaster and ski triggers ([FGS restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start); [Google AR docs](https://developers.google.com/location-context/activity-recognition)) |
| Multi-sensor capture | 100-200 Hz accelerometer and gyroscope ring buffer (10-16 s); 1 Hz GPS speed; barometer step and microphone sound level when available (no raw audio stored) | Fusion is required; the 200 Hz cap applies; barometer and microphone are optional confirming signals ([Sensors overview](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_overview); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/10/apple-offers-a-deeper-dive-into-crash-detection/)) |
| Clipping-aware features | Reads `getMaximumRange()` per device; uses velocity change, time spent at full scale, energy across axes and GPS speed drop instead of peak g | Phone sensors range from 2g to 16g and clip in crashes ([ICCS 2021](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2021/papers/127470294.pdf)) |
| Two-stage detector | Cheap trigger, then the five-check filter (speed, duration, axes, post-event stop, rotation), then a small tree or boosted model; CNN-Transformer once data allows | Cuts false alarms from ~10/h to ~0.5/h; deep models only win with large datasets ([ICCS 2021](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2021/papers/127470294.pdf); [VZCrash](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06074v1)) |
| Countdown UX | Full-screen alarm, vibration and spoken prompt in the user's language; 30-60 s; "I'm OK" / "Need help"; labels "minor crash / no crash" | Industry norm; builds a labeled false-alarm dataset as Google does ([Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)) |
| Free contacts-first escalation | Automatic on-device SMS (location link, plus code, speed, battery) plus push or server SMS in parallel; live location sharing; `ACTION_DIAL` pre-filled with the local emergency number | Only legal automatic path on Android; works without mobile data ([Play SMS policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en); [ACTION_CALL](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/content/Intent#ACTION_CALL)) |
| Reliability onboarding | Per-OEM guides for auto-start and "unrestricted" battery; battery-optimization exemption; a "protection active" status check and heartbeat | Samsung and Xiaomi score 5/5 on app killing; "Safety app" is an allowed exemption ([dontkillmyapp](https://dontkillmyapp.com/); [Doze docs](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/doze-standby)) |
| Privacy by design | On-device inference; upload only on a confirmed event; short retention; no secondary use without opt-in | EDPB, DPDP and the Texas v. Allstate/Arity lawsuit ([EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu:443/sites/default/files/consultation/edpb_guidelines_202001_connectedvehicles.pdf); [Uniqus](https://uniqus.com/digital-personal-data-protection-act-timelines/)) |
| Honest disclosure and test mode | States clearly that the app does not dial 911 itself and may miss crashes; a sensor-replay demo of the full alert flow | NENA guidance; Pixel's disclaimer precedent and demo mode ([NENA](https://www.nena.org/page/smartphoneapps); [Google Pixel Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7055029?hl=en)) |

The differentiation follows from what the incumbents structurally cannot or will not do.

- **Any Android phone.** Google's feature is limited to Pixel, Samsung's is dormant and Xiaomi's is China-only, so an app that runs on Samsung, Xiaomi, Vivo, Oppo, Realme and Motorola phones meets demand no one else is serving.
- **A two-wheeler mode.** Detection would cover lower-speed falls and skids with the phone in a pocket, not on a mount, which addresses the riders who make up 45% of Indian road deaths and whom the 25 mph car-centric products ignore.
- **Regional languages and a robust fallback.** Voice prompts in Hindi and other regional languages, roaming and dual-SIM tolerance, and an offline SMS fallback with GPS coordinates would close each gap that Pixel's help page lists.
- **No paywall on escalation.** Keeping escalation free avoids Life360's reputational damage, and monetization can come from premium extras or, more importantly, B2B channels: insurers, two-wheeler OEM and EV apps, gig platforms and fleets.
- **Dispatcher-friendly escalation.** Alerting contacts first means emergency call centers are contacted only through a human, either a family member or a partner monitoring center. That design avoids the false-alarm backlash that hit Apple.

Some items should wait until after launch: an automatic live-agent relay through a monitoring partner, Wear OS support, a paired OBD or BLE tag through Companion Device Manager (which also unlocks background starts), automatic deferral to eCall-equipped cars, and an SDK for insurers. Each one depends on detection quality the app can only prove with field data.

## Conclusion

The competitive question is less about detection accuracy than about whether users trust that protection is actually running, and whether escalation reaches someone who will act. Apple and Google have shown the algorithms can be built. What a third-party Android app must solve is everything around them: OEM battery killers, foreground-service and Play declarations, and the ban on auto-dialing emergency numbers. An app that visibly proves it is armed on a Xiaomi or Samsung phone, and alerts family within seconds even with no data connection, has something no free OEM feature offers today.

The deeper strategic point is that data compounds. VZCrash shows that labeled real-world crashes, not model design, separate a mediocre detector from a good one. A free consumer app that collects "I'm OK / minor crash / no crash" labels, along with consented crash traces from two-wheeler-heavy markets that CMT and Apple barely cover, would build the dataset the B2B side of the business needs. The consumer app is the funnel and the data engine. Insurers and platforms paying for the resulting accuracy are the business, following the path CMT and Sentiance took from a different starting point.
